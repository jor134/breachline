# BREACHLINE

Co-op tactical entry game for Quest (WebXR), phones, tablets and desktop, up to 4 officers. Everyone arrives at a training facility (shooting range with static, pop-up and moving targets, a timed shoot / no-shoot kill house, and practice doors and a dummy bomb that reset themselves); the mission board there picks one of five maps (mansion, container terminal, Grand Prix pit building with the race still running: stay off the track, and an airport: kerbside canopy, double-height departures hall with a glass gallery, security lanes, airside concourse, then across the apron to a maintenance hangar where a grounded jet holds a hostage, a gunman and a bomb in its cargo hold; the high-value target starts in the upstairs VIP lounge and runs for the jet as soon as he hears the squad; and a casino tower at dusk or night: see Casino below), the weather and, for the Grand Prix and the airport, day or night, and START sends the whole room in together. PvP: anyone can switch TEAM to TERRORIST on the board; with at least one terrorist the next mission is a PvP round (terrorist players start inside, allied with the full AI crew, can't rescue or defuse; SWAT wins by securing every hostage and bomb or taking out every terrorist, terrorists win when the timer runs out or every officer is down; one life, downed players spectate a teammate). A terrorist player can take a hostage as a human shield: free-hand grip on them in VR (USE on phone/desktop), released by letting go; the hostage covers most of the body, head and right side stay exposed; half speed and pistol only while holding; a SWAT hit or a flashbang frees the hostage, and a held hostage can't be cuffed. SWAT bots: missions fill the squad to four with AI officers (ALPHA, BRAVO, CHARLIE; three when you play alone, one fewer for each friend, and a friend who joins mid-mission takes a bot's place). They follow you, shout down anyone who hasn't complied, and shoot suspects who open fire or threaten a hostage (never an unaware or surrendered one). Point at something and open the order wheel: VR hold Y/B to point (a green laser and ring show what it will act on, with its name), let go to open the wheel, move onto an order and press Y/B or the trigger (a tap still shouts), desktop hold G and move the mouse toward an order, phone SQUAD. Orders: FOLLOW ME, HOLD, MOVE THERE (at a spot), OPEN / PICK LOCK / KICK / BREACH & CLEAR / CUT WIRE (at a door; breach kicks or opens, throws a flashbang and clears the room), DEFUSE (at a bomb), DETAIN (at a hostage or surrendered suspect), REVIVE (at a downed officer; they also revive you unasked). Bots check doors for wires and spot most of them. With nobody on the SWAT side (a solo terrorist) the bots run the mission themselves. In VR the wrist display sits on the inside of the left wrist (turn the palm up to read it), and the training board has BODY FIT to slide your own body back/forward and up/down under the headset, and all your gear (vest, belt, holsters and their grab zones) forward/back (saved on the headset). Terrorist players also carry 2 door locks and 2 wire traps: with the LOCK (lockpick slot) or TRAP (cutters slot) tool, hold USE / grip on a closed door (2 s to lock, 3 s to rig); terrorists pass their own locked and trapped doors, SWAT has to pick or kick the locks and trips the wires unless they spot and disarm them. Home-screen installs check for a new build each time they come back to the front and reload when not mid-mission. After a mission, TRAINING on the debrief returns to the range; suspects, hostages and bombs are reshuffled every mission. Dusk, with a breaker that cuts the lights, and night vision.
- **Mansion** (Operation GILDED CAGE): a three-storey modern house, walk-out basement and pool terrace above the ocean. 9 suspects, 5 hostages, 2 bombs, 12:00.
- **Terminal** (Operation IRON TIDE): a container port about 120 x 110 m. Container yard, ship-to-shore and yard cranes, trucks, an operations building (breaker), and a moored container ship: board by a gangway, cross the stacked deck, clear the bridge tower. 14 suspects, 8 hostages, 3 bombs in padlocked containers (cut the lock with the cutters, open the doors, defuse inside), 15:00. A high-value target with two bodyguards holds the bridge: arrest him for full marks.

## Deploy (GitHub + one Cloudflare Worker)
1. Cloudflare dashboard > Workers & Pages > Create > Import a repository > pick this repo.
2. Deploy command: `npx wrangler deploy` (the default). No build command, no variables.
3. Open the `*.workers.dev` URL in the Quest browser, enter a room code, press ENTER VR. Friends use the same code.

`wrangler.toml` serves `/public` as static assets and routes `/ws/<ROOM>` to a Durable Object (`Room`) that runs the mission.

## Layout
- `public/js/core/` pure game logic shared by server and client: `level.js` (walls, collision, bullets, pathfinding for any map), `maps/` (one file per map: layout, floors, spawn points), `sim.js` (suspects, hostages, bombs, rules of engagement, scoring), `weapon.js` (manual gun handling), `host.js` (message protocol).
- `public/js/` client: `game.js` (VR rig, hands, weapons, HUD, networking), `gfx.js` (shared world rendering), `maps/<id>-gfx.js` (each map's scenery and sky), `actors.js` (people, guns, bombs), `audio.js`.
- `src/worker.js` Worker + Durable Object.
- `test/` `node test/core.test.mjs`, `node test/worker.test.mjs`; `vr.py` + `xrmock.js` drive the VR path in headless Chromium with a fake headset.

## Casino

`core/maps/casino.js` + `js/maps/casino-gfx.js`, Operation HIGH ROLLER at THE ASTERION (an invented resort; no real names, logos or slot brands). The squad starts in a hovering helicopter (`spawnY`), fast-ropes to the sky-suite wing's roof, rappels three floors to four breach windows (doors with `win: true`: they only ever open, by being shot, kicked or pushed, event `shatter`), then takes the fire stairs (a scissor stair) or the service lift (`map.lift`, `Sim.liftUse` / `tickLift`, events `lift` and `liftmove`) down to the casino. Two HVTs (`map.hvts`, one report line each). Ropes are `map.ropes`, driven on the client by `js/rope.js` (VR hand over hand or left stick; phone / desktop USE plus forward / back) and on the server by `botRope` / `ropePlan` in `core/bots.js`. Furniture specific to the map (slots, tables, the round bar, fluted columns) is drawn by the map's `gfx.furn`; the CCTV wall renders one of six cameras every third frame, only while someone stands in the security office with the power on. Test: `test/vr-rope.js` (heli to roof to suite to casino by lift) and the casino tests in `core.test.mjs`.

## Character animation

All procedural (none of the models ship with clips), in `js/actors.js`:
- Officers (other players and bots) are drawn ~1.25 snapshots in the past, interpolated, so they move smoothly; their legs follow a stride cycle from the real ground velocity (forward, backward, sideways), step round when turning on the spot, crouch-walk, set each foot on the ground under it (stairs, slopes), shift weight when standing, kick doors, and fall over (knees first) and get up on a revive. Your own legs in VR step at half size.
- Suspects and hostages (the stick rig that drives their models): the same velocity-based gait with strafing and backpedalling, turning steps, crouched movement, feet on stairs, idle weight shifts and glances, guards checking weapons, trembling hostages, magazine changes, kneeling to work on someone, and a staged fall.
- Server side: suspects and bots now run dry and change magazines (`npcFire`, `botFight`); bots' hands reach for door levers, swing to throw, go low to work (cuff, revive, pick, defuse). NPC snapshot flags: 1 armed, 2 crouched, 4 reloading, 8 working.

## Tutorial

`js/tutorial.js`: a guided, hands-on tutorial. Start it from TUTORIAL on the start screen (also offered on the first visit, or `?tut=1`), or from the mission board (VR / desktop board, phone top row). Part one in the training facility (move, turn, rifle, shoot, reload, pistol, light, doors, kick, shout, tool wheel, wrist / radio menu, mission board), part two on a first mission (squad orders, arrest, finish). Each step waits until the player does it (green marker for where to go), worded for VR, phone or desktop; SKIP / EXIT always available (desktop: K). Extra lessons: body fit, night vision, grenades, locks and traps, bomb, kill house, playing with friends / PvP, level editor. VR shows a floating coach panel; phone and desktop an overlay. `howto.html` is the readable guide (controls for all three platforms, missions, squad orders, setup, online, PvP, editor).

## Level editor

`editor.html` (linked from the start screen as LEVEL EDITOR) builds maps top-down with a mouse or touch: rooms on a 0.5 m grid (up to 3 floors), doors, archways, gaps and windows on their walls, stairs between floors, furniture, the squad spawn and exit, and suspect, hostage, bomb, HVT and breaker spots. Style presets (house, office, warehouse, terminal), day or night, and uploaded textures for walls, the ground and floors. CHECK finds problems (missing markers, spots nobody can reach), TEST plays the draft solo in the game (`?draft=1`), PUBLISH puts it on the training room's mission board for everyone. Drafts save on the device; Open also lists published maps to edit, and maps can be exported and imported as JSON.

LANDSCAPE tools: a sculpt brush (raise, lower, smooth, flatten to a height or a storey) on a 1 m height grid, two storeys up or down, with slopes relaxed to at most 0.5 m per metre so the ground is always walkable. Buildings sit on the ground: inside a footprint the floor is the room's floor (foundations show where the ground falls away), so raising the ground to +1 storey beside an upper-floor door makes a walk-out. Trees (trunks stop bullets), shrubs (hide you, bullets pass), property walls on grid lines (privacy wall, low wall, wood fence that hides but bullets pass, iron railing you can see and shoot through), and gates in them that work like outside doors. All optional fields in the map JSON (`ter`, `trees`, `shrubs`, `pwalls`, `gates`); maps without them are unchanged.

Delete a published map from OPEN → Published → Delete (needs the creator code; drafts saved on the device are kept). The editor's Home button and the game's HOME SCREEN (VR/desktop menu) or HOME (phone, under MENU) go back and forth; while testing a draft it reads BACK TO EDITOR.

The format and its checks are in `js/core/maps/custom.js` (shared by the editor, the game and the Worker). Published maps and textures live in the `Library` Durable Object (SQLite) behind `/api/maps` and `/api/tex`. Publishing and uploads need the creator code: set the `CREATOR_CODE` secret on the Worker (Cloudflare dashboard > Workers > breachline > Settings > Variables and Secrets). Without it, publishing is off.

## Voice chat

Online rooms have voice chat between the human players (like Kerbside's hangouts): WebRTC audio, one connection per pair, signalled through the room's Durable Object (`js/voice.js`). Tick "Voice chat with your squad" on the start screen (remembered). The microphone is asked for on the page: in VR the first ENTER VR tap asks for it and the second enters VR, since the headset cannot show the prompt once inside. Mute: VR grab the radio for the menu, MIC button; desktop B; phone MIC. A green speaker shows over a teammate's head while they talk, and the HUD / wrist shows MIC ON, MIC OFF or NO MIC. Connections use Cloudflare STUN; set the `TURN_KEY_ID` and `TURN_KEY_API_TOKEN` secrets on the Worker (the same Cloudflare TURN key as Kerbside works) so voice also gets through strict networks via `/api/ice`.
